# Swales' CARS Model (1990) — Annotation Manual

## Section 1: Annotation of Moves and Steps

### Move 1: Establishing a Territory

The research territory (broad topic) is the background needed to understand and conduct the research. Move 1 answers: *"Why is this general research area important?"*

#### Move 1, Step 1: Claiming Centrality (CC)

The author asks the discourse community to accept the importance, impact, and centrality of the research topic.

- Sentences with linguistic markers indicating significance (adjectives like "important," "vital," nouns like "importance," "essence").
- Segments showing interest, popularity, growing attention, or usefulness of the topic (e.g., "Recently, there has been a lot of interest in…", "Many studies have focused on…").

*Examples:*

> "The researchers have stressed **the importance** of understanding academic writing…"

> "As the **demand** and participation in English-medium higher education increase, so does the expectation for students to have academic literacy skills. Hence, it is **necessary** to understand academic writing improvements…"

> "Teaching English has **become a widely researched** and discussed priority in education, and it is in the joint **interest** of stakeholders and policy makers…"

#### Move 1, Step 2: Making Topic Generalizations (MTG)

Statements about the current state of knowledge, consensus, or description of phenomena.

- Definitions of topics **without in-text citation**.
- Segments setting context or background knowledge.

*Examples:*

> "English for Specific Purposes (ESP) is a method of language teaching in which the practical needs of a group of learners shape the primary focus of the course…"

> "Writing is different to speaking, which is more spontaneous…"

**NB 1:** If the definition is accompanied by an **in-text citation**, annotate it as *Reviewing Items of Previous Research* (Move 1, Step 3) instead.

#### Move 1, Step 3: Reviewing Items of Previous Research (RIPR)

The author relates what has been found on the topic and who found it. All segments with in-text citations belong here.

*Example:*

> "**Fosnot and Perry (2005)** described constructivism as being applicable to education by postulating some distinctive practices…"

**NB 2:** If *claiming centrality* and *reviewing previous research* overlap, annotate as **Claiming Centrality**.

> "However, it **has recently become popular** and viable to carry out research into stakeholder attitudes since it is very **important** to have a dialogue between parents and teachers (Linse 2011, Nikolov and Djigunović 2011)." → CC

**NB 3:** If a reference to previous literature is used to **establish a niche**, annotate as one of the steps of Move 2.

> "Writing has been the focus of most research attention at this level of studies (Ädel, 2010; Cheng, 2008; Flowerdew, 2015), **but no research to our knowledge has focused on** the teaching and learning of academic spoken discourse…" → Move 2 (IG)

### Move 2: Establishing a Niche

The niche is the rationale for the research, pointing out gaps or problems in existing knowledge. Move 2 answers: *"What specific problems or gaps in the current knowledge does this research aim to address?"*

#### Move 2, Step 1A: Counter-Claiming (CC) — *Something is wrong*

The author refutes previous research by presenting a counterclaim.

> "**I argue that** Hidden Markov Models are unsuited to the task…"

#### Move 2, Step 1B: Indicating a Gap (IG) — *Something is missing*

The author highlights what is missing and develops the research problem around an understudied area.

> "Nonetheless, despite the fact that such units are ubiquitous in higher learning institutions in Malaysia, **little is known about** their effectiveness."

> "**To date, no studies** have empirically investigated the rhetorical structure of the introductions of applied linguistics PhD theses."

#### Move 2, Step 1C: Raising a Question or Making an Inference (RQ) — *Something is unclear*

The author highlights something unclear or ambiguous that needs additional investigation.

> "Despite prior observations of the behavior in…, **it remains unclear** why some single mothers choose to avoid…"

#### Move 2, Step 1D: Continuing a Tradition (CT) — *Adding something*

The author extends or builds on existing research.

> "Thus, **more studies are needed** to raise writers' awareness about these aspects of RAs."

**NB 4:** Distinguishing **Step 1C (RQ)** from **Step 1D (CT)**:

- If the author identifies vagueness/ambiguity ("unclear," "vague," inconsistencies, "lack of agreement"), or raises a rhetorical question inferring a gap → **Step 1C (RQ)**.
- If the author explicitly extends existing research ("Following X's work," "More research is needed," "previous studies focused on X but not Y," "To expand that theory") → **Step 1D (CT)**.

> "Not only have **the findings been inconsistent** but conclusions regarding the impact of cognitive ability…" → Step 1C (RQ).

> "**It is important to conduct more research** on this issue…" → Step 1D (CT).

---

### Move 3: Occupying the Niche

The author turns the niche into the research space they will fill. Move 3 answers: *"How do your research objectives and/or methods address the identified gap?"*

#### Move 3, Step 1A: Outlining the Purpose (OP)

Includes one or more of: **hypothesis**, **research questions**, **aims of the study**.

*Examples:*

> "**The main objective** is to identify whether cultural content has been incorporated…" (aim)

> "In order to meet this objective, we pose **two research questions**: Q1…" (research questions)

> "**It could be assumed that**, by serving the aforementioned pragma-communicative aims…" (hypothesis)

#### Move 3, Step 1B: Announcing Present Research (APR)

The author describes the current research. Annotate any segment explaining **methodology** or **rationale for the chosen methodology**.

*Examples:*

> "This study uses **a checklist** designed by the author to analyze the cultural content of six language textbooks."

> "The model is based on character-level recurrent neural networks (RNNs), which have recently seen much success in a number of morphological prediction tasks (Faruqui et al. 2016; Kann and Schütze 2016)." — methodology + citation still APR.

**NB 5:** Decisions about **methodology/structure** → APR. But rationales about **selecting the topic** belong to Move 1 (RIPR/MTG), and rationales about **implications** belong to Move 3, Step 2 (APF).

#### Move 3, Step 2: Announcing Principal Findings (APF)

The author presents a brief, general summary of key findings, or the **implications** of the study. Modality markers (*may, might, could, probably, likely*) often signal this step.

*Examples:*

> "Further, **the findings of the study might** play a great role in showing the state of today's human mind…"

> "Addressing this gap, **this study offers** a contextualized account of emergent writing practices…"

> "Furthermore, **the results of the study could** provide an assurance on the efficiency of the framework…"

**NB 6:** Modality markers can blur OP vs. APF. Default to **APF (implications)** unless the author explicitly formulates a **conditional sentence (if… then…)**, a **research question**, or a **hypothesis** (e.g., "we suppose that…", "it is hypothesized that…").

#### Move 3, Step 3: Indicating RA Structure (IRS)

States how the remainder of the paper is organized.

> "The article is divided as follows: **Section 2** presents the theoretical background… **Section 3** exemplifies… **Section 4** discusses…"

## Section 2: Segmentation

The segmentation protocol involves breaking moves into relevant steps based on their **communicative function**. **Do not leave any parts unannotated.**

### General Segmentation Rules

1. **Segment Span** — Each segment must consist of at least one complete sentence. Do not segment before a sentence terminates.
2. **Continuation of Segments** — If the next sentence semantically completes the previous one **within the same step**, include it in the same segment.
3. **Segment Termination** — Terminate a segment when there is a clear shift in moves or steps.

### Specific Segmentation Rules

#### Rule 1: Hierarchical or Chronological Indicators (Frame markers)

Treat categories split by *"first," "second," "then," "later," "finally,"* etc., as one segment. Common in MTG, RIPR, and IRS.

> "This article is structured as follows. **First**, I will provide a review… **Then**, I will introduce and discuss the present study… **Finally**, the article will be concluded…" → one segment.

#### Rule 2: Argumentation Indicators

Sentences with argumentation markers (*because, hence, so, therefore*) form one segment.

> "As the demand and participation in English-medium higher education increase, so does the expectation for students to have academic literacy skills. **Hence**, it is necessary to understand academic writing improvements…" → one segment.

#### Rule 3: Indicators Showing Exemplification (Code glosses)

Example indicators at the start of the next sentence (*for instance, for example, to exemplify, as an example, namely, such as, in other words*) continue the previous segment.

> "However, it is not evident that an early start of learning foreign languages is an advantage… **For instance**, Hanušová and Najvar (2005) investigated…" → one segment.

#### Rule 4: Anaphoric and Cataphoric References

Anaphora/cataphora indicators (pronouns, lexical cohesion, *ibid.*, *the former / the latter*) continue the segment unless the move/step changes.

> "…hedged stance can be expressed by means of that-complement clauses…, in particular extraposed that-clauses (Hyland & Tse, 2005). **The latter** are preceded by a matrix it-clause…" → one segment.

#### Rule 5: Span Continuity and Connectivity

- Connectors between sentences (*however, nevertheless, yet, but, on the other hand, besides, conversely, furthermore, correspondingly, additionally, accordingly, such, in doing so, in this regard, that's why, in other words*) continue the previous segment **only if** they semantically complete it within the same step. If the move/step shifts, start a new segment.

> "As the demand and participation in English-medium higher education increase, so is the expectation for students to have academic literacy skills. Hence, it is necessary to understand academic writing improvements that are made in academic literacy classes **(Move 1, CC)**. Furthermore, factors involved in the development of academic writing… are scarce **(Move 2, IG)**. Therefore, this study aims to address this gap. In particular, it first seeks to explore… Secondly, it will examine… **(Move 3, OP)**."

- Continue a segment as long as it stays within the same step, even if the result is a large segment.

### General Remarks

1. The moves are not necessarily in order.
2. Do not leave any parts unannotated.
